# Sea Nymph existing-model generation completion — 2026-07-31

## Scope

This is a focused completion of the five Sea Nymph records already present in BoatBuilder. It is not a full manufacturer-roster expansion. Stable boat IDs were preserved.

## Source method

The surviving year-by-year Sea Nymph rosters in JD Power/NADA were checked from 1980 through 1997. They establish annual model presence, propulsion architecture, nominal length, packaged horsepower entries and published dry weight. The 1989 package-brochure inventory independently confirms that FM161, SS175, SS195 and GLS175 were marketed together; annual roster evidence confirms GLS195 as well.

Primary annual roster pattern: `https://www.jdpower.com/boats/{year}/sea-nymph`

Secondary brochure inventory: `https://boatbrochure.com/products/sea-nymph-1989-package-brochure`

Current market calibration checked exact surviving listings including a 1993 SS175 at $7,700 with a later Evinrude E-TEC repower and a 1992 GLS195 at $5,999. Those are useful upper-market references, not proof that an original-power package deserves the same value.

The 1993 GLS175 roster identifies the model as GLS175 but shows 19 feet in its length field. BoatBuilder discloses that conflict and does not silently rewrite it into a verified hull dimension.

## Pricing and suitability

All 24 physical/evidence/configuration rows have complete-package screening ranges contained within their documented years. These ranges assume a running period-appropriate powertrain and usable trailer. Repowers, rotten floors/transoms, wet flotation, obsolete sterndrives and trailer replacement can move the real value well outside the ordinary condition bands.

Only `*GLS195 Great Lakes Special` receives the compact ideal-match marker. It is wide and Great-Lakes-oriented with a full windshield, while remaining plausibly towable and affordable. The SS195 is capable but its fish-and-ski upholstery/carpet emphasis and sterndrive ambiguity make it less ideal for Tod and Donna. The 17-foot boats are useful secondary candidates but only about 82 inches wide and lack the reserve desired for four adults on Lake Superior.
